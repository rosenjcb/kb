\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation Targets}

\textbf{Raylib.}  Our primary external benchmark is the
\href{https://github.com/raysan5/raylib}{raylib C library}—a mature,
well-documented game-programming framework (\texttt{git commit 3c82b48}).
Raylib is chosen because it is not the KB codebase itself (eliminating
evaluator familiarity bias), has rich cross-module structure suitable for
graph-based retrieval, and has stable upstream documentation.

\textbf{KB self-check.}  As a product smoke test, we also evaluate KB against
the KB codebase itself (suite \texttt{kb}).  This self-evaluation tests
whether KB can answer questions about its own architecture—a strict test of
retrieval grounding, since the evaluator has ground truth of the correct answers.

\subsection{Scoring Rubric}

Each suite covers eight questions spanning capabilities, architecture, installation,
configuration, platform specifics, conventions, gotchas, and roadmap—topics that
stress different retrieval paths through the fact graph.  Answers are scored on
four axes (0--4):

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{4.2cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Axis} & \textbf{Criterion} \\
\midrule
Correctness      & Factually grounded in the repo/KB \\
Usefulness       & Helps a developer act or understand \\
Specificity      & Uses concrete repo-specific details \\
Evidence Handling & Constrained to evidence; acknowledges uncertainty \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Success Score}
\label{subsec:success}

The headline metric is a single scalar $S \in [0,1]$ (higher is better) that
blends answer quality with the operational cost of producing it. Quality alone
rewards a system that reads the entire repository into context; $S$ penalizes
that by charging for tokens and latency:

\begin{equation}
S = w_Q \cdot Q + w_T \cdot E_{\text{tok}} + w_V \cdot E_{\text{speed}}
\label{eq:success}
\end{equation}

\noindent with default weights $w_Q = 0.60$, $w_T = 0.30$, $w_V = 0.10$. The
three components are each normalized to $[0,1]$:

\begin{align}
Q &= \frac{\bar{c} + \bar{u}}{8}, &
E_{\text{tok}} &= 1 - \min\!\Bigl(\tfrac{T}{B_T},\,1\Bigr), &
E_{\text{speed}} &= 1 - \min\!\Bigl(\tfrac{\tau}{B_\tau},\,1\Bigr)
\label{eq:success-components}
\end{align}

\noindent where $\bar{c}, \bar{u}$ are the mean correctness and usefulness axes
(each $0$--$4$), $T$ is total input$+$output tokens for the eight-question run,
and $\tau$ is total wall-clock latency. Normalization is \emph{budget-absolute}:
the references $B_T = 10^6$ tokens and $B_\tau = 6\!\times\!10^5$\,ms are fixed
rather than relative to the baseline, so $S$ is stable across comparisons. The
KB-equipped run and the control run are scored with the identical formula and
budgets, making $S$ directly comparable head-to-head; the components
$Q$, $E_{\text{tok}}$, $E_{\text{speed}}$ localize \emph{where} an advantage
arises.

\noindent\textbf{Pass criterion (secondary).}  As a diagnostic we also report
the per-question pass rate: a question passes if correctness $\geq 3$
\textbf{and} usefulness $\geq 3$, and a run is promising if $S \geq 0.70$.

\noindent\textbf{Scoring stability.}  The auto-scorer (Gemini 2.5 Flash) is
non-deterministic even at temperature 0 due to distributed inference.

\noindent\textbf{KB self-check.}  All 8 questions pass (correctness $\geq 3$
and usefulness $\geq 3$).  Evidence handling reaches 4.00/4.00 across all
questions, indicating that the retrieval loop consistently grounds answers in
indexed facts without hallucinating.  Correctness is uniformly 3/4 — accurate
but not maximally precise — suggesting the fact base covers topics
breadth-first with moderate depth.

\noindent\textbf{Raylib.}  6 of 8 questions pass.  The two failures (Q6 and
Q8) score usefulness $= 2$, indicating the answers were factually grounded but
not actionable.  Both failing questions target platform-specific build
configuration and roadmap details — topics that are sparsely represented in
raylib's markdown documentation and therefore thin in the fact graph.
Correctness on passing questions averages 3.88/4.00, and evidence handling
4.00/4.00 on those same questions, confirming retrieval quality is high where
the fact base has coverage.

Both suites exceed the 0.70 pass-rate threshold.  The raylib graph is
substantially larger (14,087 entities, 12,744 edges) reflecting the C
codebase's dense header structure; the KB self-check graph (2,216 entities,
772 edges) is smaller but achieves a higher pass rate, consistent with
the self-referential nature of the evaluation.

\noindent\textbf{Success score.}  The quality components are $Q = 0.86$
(KB self-check) and $Q = 0.92$ (raylib).  Under the success score
(Eq.~\ref{eq:success}), these quality terms contribute $w_Q \cdot Q = 0.52$
and $0.55$ of the maximum $0.60$ quality weight; the remaining $0.40$ is
governed by token economy and latency, which the schema~v2 telemetry capture
records for both the KB-equipped and control runs so that $S$ can be compared
head-to-head.  Because normalization is budget-absolute, a system that
inflates context to raise $Q$ is penalized through $E_{\text{tok}}$, making
$S$ a more faithful proxy for practical retrieval value than quality alone.
